A Lightning wallet you host yourself and own outright.

Allod runs next to your own Bitcoin and Lightning node and gives you a web wallet, a REST API and Nostr Wallet Connect. No sign-up, no company between you and your money. Open source under the MIT license.

Get started Read the source

Allodium, in medieval law: land held in absolute ownership, owing rent and service to no lord. That is the idea, applied to a Lightning node.

Everything runs on a machine you control.

One Docker Compose bundle starts the whole stack on a VPS, an Umbrel or a small computer at home. Allod is the top layer: the wallet, the accounts and the connections to your apps.

Domains and gateways, managed for you

Bring a domain or buy one from Settings, and Allod manages its DNS records with DNSSEC. If your node has no public address, rent a gateway and pay for it over Lightning: HTTPS passes through a WireGuard tunnel without being decrypted on the way, and your home address never appears in DNS.

A gateway renews itself from your wallet before it expires, up to a maximum you set.

Who your node depends on.

A self-hosted wallet is only as independent as the services behind it. These are the questions worth asking, with Allod's answers.

Where does chain data come from?
Your own Bitcoin Core. A pruned node that checks every block itself and is synced in a few hours. No outside server tells you what your balance is.
Who checks the Lightning graph?
Your own gossip server. It verifies each channel against your own chain before your node uses it for routing.
If you use a Lightning address, who answers the payer?
Your node, on your own domain. Nobody in between can hand out their own invoice instead of yours. Hosted addresses are supported too, and Allod shows what you give up before you pick one.
Who owns your name?
You do. Lightning address, BIP-353 and NIP-05 names live on a domain you bring. Allod keeps the DNS records up to date, and you can buy the domain from inside the wallet.
Who delivers your notifications?
Your node. It sends each push itself, encrypted end to end. With UnifiedPush and your own ntfy server, the whole path is self-hosted.
How do you sign in?
With your Nostr signer. No password and no account to create anywhere.
How is your money tracked?
On a double-entry ledger. Every movement is a balanced pair, across all accounts and connected apps.
Can you use BOLT12 everywhere?
Yes. Send and receive in the web wallet, the API and over Nostr Wallet Connect.

Get started on a VPS.

You need a Debian or Ubuntu machine with a 50 GB disk. The installer adds Docker only if it is missing.

  1. Run the installer

    It asks for the network, the chain source, a domain, Tor and backups, then writes the settings. As root it installs to /opt/allod, as any other user to ~/allod. It downloads signed images, builds nothing and starts nothing.

    curl -fsSL https://allod.me/install.sh | sh

    To read the script before you run it:

    curl -fsSLO https://allod.me/install.sh
less install.sh
sh install.sh

  2. Start the stack

    The installer prints these commands with your install directory filled in.

    cd ~/allod    # /opt/allod if you installed as root
docker compose pull
docker compose up -d

  3. Open the setup link

    This prints a one-time link. Without a domain the wallet listens on 127.0.0.1:8029 only, so reach it through an SSH tunnel or the Tor address. With a domain, use --base-url https://your.domain.

    docker compose exec allod allod setup-link --base-url http://127.0.0.1:8029

  4. Connect your signer and write down the phrase

    The wizard connects a Nostr signer, which becomes the owner key, then creates or restores the recovery phrase. The Lightning node starts once Bitcoin has synced. After that, open a channel and you can pay and receive.

Get started on umbrelOS.

Allod is not in the official Umbrel App Store. You add this repository as a community app store, then install from it. No terminal needed.

  1. Open Community App Stores

    In umbrelOS, open the App Store, click the … menu at the top right and choose Community App Stores.

  2. Add the Allod store

    Paste this address and click Add, then open the Allod store that appears.

    https://github.com/frnandu/allod

  3. Install Allod

    Click Install and open Allod from its tile. If the Umbrel Bitcoin Node app is installed, Allod uses it. If not, Allod runs its own pruned Bitcoin Core (up to 50 GB) and needs time to sync first.

  4. Connect your signer and write down the phrase

    The setup wizard opens straight from the tile: no link, no token. Connect a Nostr signer, which becomes the owner key, then create or restore the recovery phrase. Details: Umbrel notes.

Get started with the ISO.

A bootable image for a PC (BIOS or UEFI) or a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5. It is a proof of concept. The system runs from the USB stick or SD card, and the wallet data goes on a separate disk, so use an SSD you can dedicate to Allod.

  1. Download the image

    Get allod-amd64.iso (PC, about 830 MB) or allod-rpi-arm64.img.xz (Raspberry Pi) from the latest GitHub release, together with SHA256SUMS-appliance. Check the file before you write it:

    sha256sum -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS-appliance

  2. Write it to a USB stick or SD card

    Use balenaEtcher or Raspberry Pi Imager ("Use custom"), or dd on Linux. Replace /dev/sdX with your stick and check it first: everything on it is erased.

    sudo dd if=allod-amd64.iso of=/dev/sdX bs=4M conv=fsync status=progress

  3. Boot it with a blank data disk

    Plug in a blank SSD of at least 10 GB (50 GB is better), boot from the stick (Raspberry Pi: SD card, then a USB SSD or NVMe) and wait. Allod formats the largest blank disk for its data on its own, and never the one it booted from. A disk with data on it is left alone and you are asked what to do (Ctrl+Alt+F2). Nothing is downloaded at boot.

  4. Open the wallet

    With a screen attached, a kiosk opens the wallet and you sign in there. Otherwise browse to http://allod.local:8029 or http://<ip>:8029 from another computer on your network. Then connect your signer and write down the recovery phrase.
