A Lightning wallet you host yourself and own outright.

Allod runs next to your own Bitcoin and Lightning node and gives you a web wallet, a REST API and Nostr Wallet Connect. No sign-up, no company between you and your money. Open source under the MIT license.

Get started Read the source

Allodium, in medieval law: land held in absolute ownership, owing rent and service to no lord. That is the idea, applied to a Lightning node.

Everything runs on a machine you control.

One Docker Compose bundle starts the whole stack on a VPS, an Umbrel or a small computer at home. Allod is the top layer: the wallet, the accounts and the connections to your apps.

Web walletIn the browser, or installed as an app on your phone
REST APIFor your own scripts and services
Nostr Wallet ConnectFor any app that speaks NIP-47

Nothing in this picture belongs to a vendor. There is no account to create, no license server and no telemetry.

What you can do with it.

Pay and get paid
BOLT11 invoices and BOLT12 offers, hold invoices, and on-chain sends and receives. Send to a Lightning address, an LNURL, a BIP-353 name, or someone you follow on Nostr.
Activity shows who a payment came from: zaps, payer notes and comments.
Connect your apps
Nostr Wallet Connect with the full NIP-47 method set. Each connection gets its own permissions, spending budget and expiry, and never sees another app's payments.
Connections run over several relays at once, so an app reaches your wallet as long as one relay is up.
Separate accounts
Sub-wallets on one node for family members, projects or apps. Each has its own balance, statement, connections and addresses, and sees nothing of the others.
A double-entry ledger records every sat. A payment counts as settled or failed only when the node confirms it.
Names on your own domain
Lightning addresses, BIP-353 names and NIP-05 on a domain you bring, with DNSSEC. Allod checks a name against DNS before it calls it live.
Prisms
An address can forward a share of every payment it receives to other Lightning targets. Each payout keeps its own state, so one that fails is retried and stays visible until it arrives or you clear it.
Notifications from your node
Allod sends each push itself, encrypted end to end, to your browser or installed web app. With UnifiedPush and your own ntfy server, the whole path is self-hosted.
Sign in with your Nostr key
A browser extension or a remote signer app is your login. There is no password to leak, and the node restarts without an unlock step.
Run the node
Open and close channels, manage peers and on-chain funds, and switch the chain source between Bitcoin Core, Electrum and Esplora from Settings.

Where the others lead

Allod is in early development, and the projects above are mature and in daily use. Alby Hub supports several Lightning engines, has an app store and ships native mobile push today. LNbits has a large set of extensions. BTCPay Server has point of sale and store plugins. phoenixd manages channels for you. Allod runs on ldk-server only, has one owner per instance, and leaves keysend out by design.

Get started.

You need a Linux machine with Docker and Compose v2. A pruned Bitcoin node fits on an 80 GB disk.

  1. Start the stack

    Clone the repository, copy the example settings and bring it up. Compose builds the images from source the first time, so this step takes a while.

    git clone https://github.com/allod-me/allod.git
cd allod/deploy
cp .env.example .env    # network, chain mode, passwords
docker compose up -d

  2. Open the setup link

    This prints a one-time link. The wallet listens on 127.0.0.1:8029 only, so reach it through an SSH tunnel, or turn on the tor or https profile in .env.

    docker compose exec allod allod setup-link --base-url http://127.0.0.1:8029

  3. Connect your signer and write down the phrase

    The wizard connects a Nostr signer, which becomes the owner key, then creates or restores the recovery phrase. The Lightning node starts once Bitcoin has synced. After that, open a channel and you can pay and receive.

Early development. Try Allod on signet or testnet4 first (ALLOD_NETWORK=signet) and use amounts you can afford to lose on mainnet. Allod trusts the machine it runs on and has no unlock password, so use full-disk encryption on hardware you own. On umbrelOS, add the repository as a community app store instead; see the Umbrel guide.
